# Transplanted hearts take the recipient’s biological age

Added 2026-10-06 from @scitechgirl. Preprint. Not peer reviewed.

## Paper

- Poganik JR et al. *Transplanted hearts assimilate the recipient’s biological age.* bioRxiv. 17 Sep 2026.
- DOI: 10.64898/2026.09.15.751836
- Brigham and Women’s / Harvard.

## What they report

Mouse heterotopic heart grafts: old hearts in young hosts looked younger on DNA methylation and gene expression; young hearts in old hosts aged faster. The shift stayed in the graft. The host’s own age clock did not move the other way. A small human transplant set (age gaps of about 8–24 years one way and 38–50 the other) showed the same direction: graft biological age tracked the recipient more than the donor.

## Limit

Preprint. Mouse grafts were extra hearts, not orthotopic replacements. Human n is small. Not a rejuvenation therapy. It says the body’s systemic environment writes on the organ.

## Why it is in the arsenal

If the host environment sets organ age, then systemic repair (the rest of this list) may matter more than swapping in a young part. Also a possible argument for using older donor hearts in younger recipients. Needs peer review and a larger cohort.
