# Johns Hopkins protein switch — cancer cell as its own prodrug factory

Added 2026-10-06. Source post: https://x.com/scitechgirl/status/2107497809297428539

The post dates this "2026" and cites only "Johns Hopkins University." That date is wrong. The method it describes is Marc Ostermeier and James R. Eshleman, Johns Hopkins, published about 2011 and improved in 2012. A reply on the post points at PMC5821872.

## What it is

A fusion protein. One domain binds the cancer-associated marker HIF-1alpha (via the CH1 domain of p300). The other is yeast cytosine deaminase, which converts the inactive prodrug 5-fluorocytosine into 5-fluorouracil. When HIF-1alpha is present, the switch turns on and the cancer cell makes the poison inside itself. Tested in human colon (RKO) and breast (MCF7) cancer cells in culture. Later variants (Haps59 and mutants from circular permutation) widened the gap between on and off.

EurekAlert summary: https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/813841

## Why it is in the arsenal

Same doctrine as BENEIN: do the damage inside the sick cell, not in the neighborhood. A protein switch is a delivery idea, not a new toxin.

## Do not confuse with DRP-104

Johns Hopkins also has DRP-104 (sirpiglenastat), a glutamine-antagonist prodrug of DON, licensed to Dracen and in early solid-tumor trials. That is a small molecule, not this protein switch. Different program.

## Limit

Cell culture. No animal cure, no trial, no 2026 paper matching the post. Selective delivery of the switch gene or protein was the unsolved step even then.
