# 15-PGDH inhibition and cartilage regeneration

Saved 2026-10-05. Research note. Not a medicine. Not a dosing guide.

## Source

- Singla M, Wang YX, Monti E, Bedi Y, Agarwal P, Su S, Ancel S, Hermsmeier M, Devisetti N, Pandey A, Afshar Bakooshli M, Palla AR, Goodman S, Blau HM, Bhutani N.
- *Inhibition of 15-hydroxy prostaglandin dehydrogenase promotes cartilage regeneration.* Science. 2025 Nov 27;391(6789):1053-1062.
- DOI: 10.1126/science.adx6649. PMID: 41308124.
- Senior authors: Helen M. Blau and Nidhi Bhutani, Stanford University.
- Inhibitor used in the paper: SW033291, a small-molecule 15-PGDH inhibitor.

## What the study found

15-PGDH (a prostaglandin-degrading enzyme Blau’s group calls a gerozyme) is about twice as abundant in aged mouse knee cartilage as in young cartilage.

Systemic or local inhibition regenerated articular cartilage in:

- aged mice with natural cartilage loss
- young adult mice after an ACL-type injury (post-traumatic osteoarthritis)
- human osteoarthritis joint explants taken at replacement surgery

The new tissue was hyaline cartilage (collagen II and the right proteoglycans), not the weaker fibrocartilage that usually fills a joint injury. Pain-related behavior fell in the mouse models. Human explants became stiffer and less inflamed.

Mechanism: existing chondrocytes changed gene expression. Hypertrophic, 15-PGDH-high cells decreased. Matrix-building articular chondrocytes increased. Stem or progenitor cells were not the driver. Blau: “We were looking for stem cells, but they are clearly not involved.”

Injury protocol in the Stanford release: injections twice a week for four weeks after the ACL-type tear sharply reduced later osteoarthritis in mice.

## What it is not

- Not a human cartilage trial. No person has regrown a knee from this paper.
- Not a Japanese pill, and not the UCLA ARPA-H joint-injection program. Those are separate tracks.
- The molecule in the paper (SW033291) is a tool compound. A related oral 15-PGDH inhibitor has Phase 1 muscle-weakness data in healthy volunteers (Epirium class). Cartilage is a hoped-for next trial, not an approved use.

## Why it is in the arsenal

Same doctrine as the other repair entries: block an aging enzyme, wake the cells already in the tissue, get hyaline cartilage instead of scar. Mobility after 70 is a death-adjacent pathway (falls, frailty, lost walking). Pairs with the muscle 15-PGDH work already on the list. Needs a randomized human joint trial before it is a medicine.
